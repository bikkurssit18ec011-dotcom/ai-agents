## The angle

KSFE's problem is not awareness, it is **competitive digital share of voice**.
Muthoot, Manappuram and private chit operators run continuous paid social and
search in Kerala and in the Gulf. KSFE has the one advantage none of them can
claim — **it is government-owned and state-guaranteed** — and it is
under-marketed on exactly the channels where that message converts.

Three concrete openings:

| Opportunity | Why it wins |
|---|---|
| **Pravasi Chitty / Gulf NRI** | Highest-ticket subscriber, geo-targetable (UAE/Saudi/Qatar/Oman), Malayalam-language creative, and KSFE already has an online registration flow to optimise against. This is the strongest single pitch. |
| **Branch-level geo campaigns** | 33+ TVM branches = 33 radius-targeted campaigns with distinct catchments (Technopark salaried at Kazhakuttom, handloom traders at Balaramapuram, tourism/NRI at Varkala). |
| **Offline conversion attribution** | The genuinely hard problem: proving a branch walk-in came from an ad. Solve this and you own the account, because no incumbent is doing it. |

**Compliance line to include** — it separates you from generic agencies:
all creative avoids implied or guaranteed returns, per the Chit Funds Act 1982
and ASCI financial-advertising codes.

## Sending rules

- **Verify addresses first.** Most `<code>@ksfe.com` values in the CSV are
  derived from KSFE's naming convention, not scraped. Validate before sending;
  bounces at scale damage your sending domain.
- Send from a **business domain**, never a free mailbox — you are writing to a
  government institution.
- Stagger sends; do not hit RO and 33 branches the same morning.
- Phone beats email for PSUs. Use the landline numbers in the CSV, 10:30–16:00
  on working days, and treat email as the follow-up that leaves a paper trail.
- Log every reply against `branch_code` so the CSV stays the single source of truth.
